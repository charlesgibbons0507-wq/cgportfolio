01 / My story

Long before I knew the word engineering, I was already testing how systems worked.

At six years old, I would speed-dial my mother’s work phone. When she didn’t answer, I changed the last four digits just to see what would happen—and ended up calling my way around the office. Her coworkers soon gave me a nickname: “Speed Dial.”

That curiosity never left. By eight, computers and online games had introduced me to digital worlds and the systems behind them. Growing up on a computer meant learning to troubleshoot whenever something broke—modifying files, working with scripts, and solving problems across both hardware and software. At twelve, I built my first computer and learned how every component had to belong, connect, and work together. As a teenager, when professional repairs cost more than I could afford, I learned to replace cracked iPhone screens and broken components myself. Years of exploring and repairing technology gave me a natural affinity for understanding how it works and the confidence to take complicated systems apart, learn them, and make them better.

I have always been a critical thinker. I naturally look for patterns, question assumptions, and test what happens when one variable changes. That instinct directly influences how I solve problems: I begin with the human need, follow the evidence, explore different paths, and keep refining until the solution works as a complete experience.

“Curiosity shows me how things work.
Creativity helps me find a better way forward.”

My foundation in information systems helps me move comfortably between product thinking, software development, and analytics. I use AI as a practical collaborator for faster iteration—while keeping judgment, testing, and the customer’s actual problem at the center.
